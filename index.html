The Soul of Product Management

Twenty-three years ago, I connected my first operational system to the internet: a cattle temperature sensor. Today, I've deployed integrated OT solutions across hundreds of facilities—from intelligent building automation systems that slash energy consumption by 40% to smart lighting platforms that adapt to human behavior to connected plumbing infrastructure that prevents water waste.

"Great products aren't born from technology. They're born from obsessing over problems that keep people awake at night."

This journey began with a simple realization: Product management isn't about managing products—it's about managing the problems our products solve.

The Problem-First Philosophy

Across my career in operational and smart infrastructure, I’ve learned that success rarely comes from the most advanced technology—it comes from solving the right problem. Time and again, I’ve seen how focusing on the real pain points behind the can turn complexity into clarity and ideas into outcomes.

I've watched countless products fail not because the product was wrong, but because teams fell in love with features that impressed peers rather than solved customer problems.
That’s when the core lesson came: Product management is about solving people’s problems, not adding features for their own sake. Lose sight of that, and the product just grows more complex and costly.

I shifted my mindset: Spend the time to truly understand the problem before crafting a solution.
Once, I pitched real-time asset tracking to a hospital. The chief doctor replied, “Nurses actually appreciate the downtime when searching; you’d just make the job harder.” That day, I realized I was solving the wrong problem.

Breakthroughs happened by listening: A major utility struggled with our wireless street lighting interface. Adding a simple optical transceiver—just like the meters they knew—clinched an $18M win.

This philosophy drives every decision: Whether building LightGrid at GE or launching $100M+ portfolios at Philips and Siemens, it’s always about one thing—What’s the real problem, and who are we helping?

1Deep Customer Empathy

I don't just gather requirements—I spend time in the field. I've commissioned systems at 3 AM, debugged network issues in freezing parking lots, and sat through countless customer pain-point sessions. The best product insights come from being uncomfortably close to customer reality.

2Technology-Business Translation

Having an engineering background allows me to speak both languages fluently. I can evaluate whether a ZigBee mesh network or 6LoWPAN is the right choice while simultaneously building business cases that resonate with C-suite executives.

3Execution Excellence

Great product strategy is worthless without flawless execution. I've learned to navigate supply chain disruptions, lead cross-functional teams through complex technical integrations, and manage product lifecycles from concept to scale.

Working across operational (OT) leaders like Philips, GE, Siemens, and Johnson Controls taught me something crucial: the future belongs to integrated OT platforms that seamlessly bridge physical operations with digital intelligence.

I've seen billion-dollar companies struggle because their brilliant engineers created solutions looking for problems, rather than solving real customer pain points. My approach? Start with the customer's midnight anxiety:

  • Facility managers losing sleep over energy costs? → Energy Dashboard and analytics
  • Healthcare facilities worried about infection control? → Smart restroom solutions with real-time monitoring
  • Building operators overwhelmed by complex systems? → Low-code/no-code integration platforms
  • Cities struggling with aging infrastructure? → Smart city solutions for outdoor lighting and traffic optimization
